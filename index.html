PicoVox

PicoVox - realistic speech synthesis in pure JavaScript, under 1 MB.

The paper: PicoVox: Realistic Neural Speech Synthesis in Under 1 MB of JavaScript - how PicoVox's networks are designed, trained, distilled, and evaluated.

PicoVox speaks English text with twelve natural-sounding male and female voices, entirely on your device - in the browser or in Node. There is no recorded speech, no server, no WebAssembly, and no runtime dependencies: a small grapheme-to-phoneme network pronounces every word, a small neural network (283K parameters) predicts interpretable source-filter synthesizer parameters every 5 ms, and a JavaScript synthesizer with an LF glottal source renders them. The whole package - code and both networks - is under 1 MB.

PicoShelf, every public-domain book read aloud right in your browser, is built on PicoVox.

Installation

npm install picovox

PicoVox is ESM-only. It runs in current browsers (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari 16.4 or later) and in Node 22 or later.

Quick start

In the browser - speak text aloud with Web Audio (call it from a click, so the browser lets the audio start):

<button id="speak">Speak</button>
<script type="module">
    import {speak} from './node_modules/picovox/lib/index.js';

    document.getElementById('speak').onclick = () => {
        speak('It was the best of times, it was the worst of times.', {'voice': 'female'});
    };
</script>

(With a bundler or an import map, import from 'picovox'.)

In Node - synthesize speech and write a WAV file:

import {synthesize, toWav} from 'picovox';
import {writeFileSync} from 'node:fs';

const {samples, sampleRate} = await synthesize('Call me Ishmael.', {'voice': 'male'});
writeFileSync('ishmael.wav', toWav(samples, sampleRate));

Streaming - a sentence and a fraction of a second at a time, the first speech in about 70 ms (M3 Max):

import {stream} from 'picovox';

for await (const block of stream(longText, {'voice': 'female', 'rate': 1.2})) {
    // block is a Float32Array of 24 kHz samples - play it, or post it from a Web Worker
}

API

synthesize(text, options)

Synthesize speech. Returns a Promise of {samples, sampleRate} - samples is a Float32Array of mono samples (-1 to

  1. at sampleRate Hz.

stream(text, options)

Synthesize speech as a stream - an async iterable of Float32Array blocks of samples, made as they're taken. The text is spoken a sentence at a time, each sentence a fraction of a second at a time, so the first audio comes almost at once. The blocks, concatenated, are exactly synthesize's samples. Synthesis runs on the calling thread as each block is taken - in a browser, take the blocks in a Web Worker to keep the page responsive.

speak(text, options)

Speak text aloud in a browser, with Web Audio. Returns a playback, {stop(), finished} - stop() stops it, and finished is a Promise that resolves when the playback ends or is stopped (and rejects if synthesis fails). Playback starts with the first blocks while the rest are synthesized, a few seconds ahead. Synthesis runs on the main thread, a block at a time between the page's other work; for long texts, use stream in a Web Worker and play its blocks. options.context is an AudioContext to play in (by default, a new one at the sample rate, closed when the playback ends). Play blocks in a context at their sample rate (new AudioContext({'sampleRate': 24000})): a context at another rate resamples each block apart, and the blocks' joins click. Under Node, speak throws an error - there is no Web Audio; use synthesize and toWav.

voices()

The built-in voices - [{key, label, sex, description}], the default first:

Key Label Sex Character

female Female female A modern, confident, and friendly female voice.
sarah Sarah female A cheerful female voice with real resonance under it, personable and decisive.
helen Helen female A low, grounded female voice, quick to the point, with a sincere tone.
amy Amy female A young, playful, and upbeat female voice.
kate Kate female A settled, even-toned, and kind female voice with crisp consonants.
grace Grace female A rounded, soft-edged female voice with an easy, relaxed delivery.
james James male A resonant, decisive male voice with a wide dynamic range.
robert Robert male A trim, focused male voice with a steady, low-set core.

david David male A punchy, close male voice with crisp consonants and a pitch that lifts across each line.
thomas Thomas male A high-energy, encouraging male voice.

Each voice is one row of the voice model's speaker table, learned from one voice of the local TTS model that speaks make train's training speech (train/data/train.json's tts voices - see the training speech); test/voices.json is their design record. The voices are the model's speakers as it speaks them - no blends, and no pitch, tract, or rate adjustments of their own - except that every voice's source is 5 dB darker than the network predicts it (lib/synthesis.js sourceShelf): in blind A/B listening (1.6.6) the network's source sounded tinny, and darker it was less tinny and never less natural. On the listener panel's texts the voices score a mean UTMOS of 4.18-4.41 (4.31 over the twelve) and a Whisper word error rate of 0.6-1.5% (1.13%) - the voices before 2.0 scored 4.13 and 1.36%, and a blind A/B preferred the new voices in all 15 trials.

styles()

The speaking styles - [{key, label, kind, description}], neutral (the default) first. Any style goes with any voice:

Key Kind
neutral (the default) emotion The voice as designed - plain narration
angry emotion A pressed, raised, louder voice
happy emotion A brighter, much higher voice
sad emotion A quieter, lower, flatter, and slower voice
fearful emotion A breathy, quieter, much higher, and quicker voice
disgusted emotion A pressed, quieter, lower, and much slower voice
whisper mode No voicing - the breath only
soft mode A quieter voice, a little slower
shouting mode A pressed, bright, raised voice, a little louder
dialogue mode Neutral dialogue - a little higher, brighter, and quicker than the narration
exclaim mode An exclamation - the dialogue style livelier: louder, wider in pitch, and quicker

Every style transforms the synthesizer's parameters (lib/transforms.js - a pressed or relaxed glottal pulse, pitch and pitch range, whisper, level, frication) and the pace; the voice model has no style input. The emotions' transforms are measured: before 2.0 the voice model had emotion embeddings learned from CREMA-D's actors, and happy, sad, fearful, and disgusted are those embeddings' effects on the parameter frames (pitch, pitch range, pulse, voicing, frication, level, and duration, over the panel texts in every voice), less the breathiness they added; angry was transforms alone already. On the listener panel's texts, in the female and male voices, every style stays intelligible (Whisper's word error rate 0.6-3.0%, whispering 2.4-3.9%); UTMOS - a judge of plain read speech - scores every voiced style within 0.05 of neutral's (4.40 and 4.26) but fearful (0.08 and 0.15 below), and can't score a whisper; and every style but whisper (6.5-9.7 dB quieter) and soft (5 dB) is within 4.1 dB of neutral's loudness - angry 3.3-3.8 dB louder, sad 3.4-3.6 quieter.

Expressive reading: style: 'auto'

Style 'auto' reads a text as a narrator does - its delivery follows the text's structure:

  • The dialogue beat. The quotations (between double quotation marks) are read in the dialogue style - a little higher, brighter, and quicker than the narration (pitch +7%, pace +5%) - and the narration neutral.
  • The exclamation beat. A quoted sentence ending in "!" is read in the exclaim style - livelier than the dialogue beat: 3 dB louder, its pitch range 20% wider, a little more pressed and quicker - excited, not shouted.
  • The paragraph beat. Each paragraph is read as one arc: its first sentence lifted (pitch +8%, 2 dB louder), the pitch settling to 4% below the voice's by its last sentence, which is 7% slower.

Style 'auto' needs no model - it reads the text's quotation marks, exclamation marks, and paragraphs. (A small network that would read a quotation angry, shouting, or fearful was tried and dropped: even a classifier on a 4-billion-parameter embedding model couldn't choose those styles reliably enough - see the paper's Section 12.7.)

On the listener panel's texts the beats keep the voices intelligible and natural: in all twelve voices style 'auto''s UTMOS is within 0.04 of neutral's, its word error rate at most 2.4% (neutral's 0.6-1.8%), and it reads about 2.3 dB louder. The output is deterministic, as every style's; without 'auto' the audio is the same as before, bit for bit.

toWav(samples, sampleRate)

Encode samples (a Float32Array, or an array of numbers, -1 to 1 - values beyond are clipped) as a 16-bit PCM mono WAV file - a Uint8Array of the file's bytes.

Options

synthesize, stream, and speak take the same options:

Option Default
voice 'male' The voice's key - see voices()
style 'neutral' The speaking style's key - see styles() - or 'auto', expressive reading (below)
rate 1 The speaking speed, from 0.5 to 2 (1.2 is 20% faster)
seed 1000 The noise seed (an integer) - sentence i of the text is rendered with seed + i
sampleRate 24000 The output sample rate (Hz), from 8000 to 96000 - 24 kHz is the voices' own rate

The text is split into sentences (and paragraphs, at blank lines); each sentence starts with a short pause - 0.24 seconds, or 0.4 before a paragraph (so the text's first sentence starts with one). Numbers (four-digit numbers as years, 1972 as "nineteen seventy-two"; ordinals, 1st; decades, 1970s; percentages; money, $1.50 and $5 million; times, 10:30 a.m.), common abbreviations, dashes, ellipses, and Roman numerals (Chapter IV, World War II) are spoken as words, and an aside's parentheses pause as a comma does. An invalid text or option throws a TypeError or a RangeError with a message saying what's wrong (stream and speak throw at once; synthesize rejects).

The output is bit-for-bit deterministic: the same text and options give the same samples, in the browser and in Node.

How the models are trained

Both networks are trained from scratch by make train, which rebuilds lib/models/g2pModel.js and lib/models/voiceModel.js from a clean checkout. The voice network learns from training speech that make train generates: Project Gutenberg sentences spoken by a local TTS model (Gradium's Phonon, through Ollama - one of its voices for each of the package's twelve), aligned and checked with our own phoneme recognizer, then a large teacher model trained through a differentiable copy of the synthesizer, distilled to the small student that ships. The G2P network learns from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary (downloaded, pinned; it is not used at runtime), its heteronyms' readings measured in the training speech. The whole run takes about 85 minutes on an M3 Max (the training speech generated once, then cached - generating and aligning it the first time adds about an hour and a half). See make train below, and the paper, PicoVox: Realistic Neural Speech Synthesis in Under 1 MB of JavaScript, for the methods and results.

PicoShelf

PicoShelf is the showcase app - an audiobook player for every public-domain book (Project Gutenberg and Standard Ebooks), read aloud by PicoVox in the browser, in a Web Worker (source).

Development

This repository is the package (lib/, test/) and the training pipeline that makes its models (train/, tools/, the paper in paper/). The package is developed with javascript-build, and the training's Python code with python-build:

make commit      # the package's unit tests (100% coverage), lint, and API documentation (jsdoc) - and the training's gate
make test        # the package's unit tests (TEST='name' runs one)
make -C train commit      # the training's gate alone - its unit tests, pylint, and 100% branch coverage
make -C train test TEST=tests.test_params    # one test module of the training's
make -j train             # train both models - the G2P and the voice - each gated, in build/train/, then a report (see below)
make train-check          # the package's tests with the trained models, then the blind A/B of the trained voice
make train-install        # the trained models that differ into lib/models/ (train-install-voice, -g2p: one model)
make -j train-voice       # the voice alone; make -j train-g2p - the G2P alone, on every core

The training pipeline is a Python package, picovox_train (train/src/picovox_train/ - picovox_train/<module>.py below), and Node scripts (tools/); they use the package's modules but aren't part of it. train/ is a python-build package of its own: its unit tests (train/src/tests/) run on the CPU in a few minutes, with tiny synthetic data and no network, in python-build's environment (train/build/venv/), which installs the package with the training's pinned PyTorch and numpy (train/pyproject.toml, the same versions as train/requirements.txt). make train runs the package from its source (PYTHONPATH=train/src) in its environment, build/venv-train from train/requirements.txt - the training (PyTorch, soxr, Praat, and the phoneme recognizer's transformers), the listener models, and spaCy (picovox_train/corpora.py's downloads need only the standard library). A training module runs the same way by hand:

PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.smoke   # every training entry point, a few steps on the CPU

Command-line tools

node tools/unpack.js lib/models/voiceModel.js build/voice                 # a packaged model back to its export
node tools/speak.js -m build/voice "Hello, world." -o build/hello.wav     # an exported model (<voice>.json and .bin)
node tools/packagecheck.js lib/models/voiceModel.js build/voice           # check a packaged model against its export
node tools/heteronyms.js                                                # the heteronym and names regression sets

The performance lab, tools/perf/, times the playback pipeline on a phone: node tools/perf/server.js serves the repository on the local network (open the printed URL on the phone, which then polls for commands), and node tools/perf/send.js COMMAND '{options}' runs one there and prints its result - timing (the network, the renderer, and the streaming pipeline, stage by stage, in a worker as an app runs them), parallel and pipeline (several workers, or network and render workers in pairs, at once), bench (fixed workloads - whether the JavaScript engine compiles them; an iPhone's Safari interprets JavaScript on a page served over plain http, as the lab's is - so the lab measures that case; over https it compiles), and reload. A variant of lib/ to try a change in is tools/perf/variant.sh NAME (build/perf/variants/NAME/lib, which the lab's commands take as lib); tools/perf/compare.js NAME compares its output with lib/'s (identical, else the SNR), and tools/perf/speed.js lib NAME times each under Node without a JIT (--jitless - the phone's speed within about 20%). --root DIR serves another app (PicoShelf, with its ?timing&log=URL timeline posted to build/perf/devlog.jsonl).

tools/speak.js speaks with an exported model (picovox_train.export CHECKPOINT --voice NAME writes train/data/NAME.json and .bin; tools/unpack.js makes one of the package's multi-speaker model), every word pronounced by the G2P network as the package pronounces (-r rate, -s seed, --params the parameter frames, --rate the duration multiplier, --batch a list of items); -m also takes a packaged module (lib/models/voiceModel.js). The multi-speaker model takes a speaker (--speaker, an id of its manifest's speakers - one per voice, in voices()'s order: 0 is male, the default); the voices' source shelf and the styles are lib/synthesis.js's, not the tool's.

The training dashboard, tools/dashboard/, follows make train as it runs: serve the repository (python3 -m http.server) and open http://localhost:8000/tools/dashboard/ (with ?train=DIR for a run in another TRAIN_DIR - another checkout's or worktree's; default build/train). It has three tabs (each linkable, e.g. #listen): Train, Listen, and Logs. Train is the make train run (the default tab while it runs): the run's state, its ETA and critical path; the CPU and GPU utilization over the run with each stage as a band (the GPU's idle time shaded, the forecast faded); every stage of train/data/train-stages.json in collapsible groups - its state (waiting, queued for the GPU, running, done, failed, stopped, or not in the run's make target, from TRAIN_DIR/stages.jsonl), its start, end, and time against the expected time, and its mean CPU and GPU use; the training runs (TRAIN_DIR/runs/, their log.jsonl and train.log) with charts of all their measurements - losses, validation, pitch error, and step times; and the gates (the heteronym and G2P checks, the listener panel's scores, and the pick). Listen plays the make train run's listening samples (each training stage's final model beside the shipped model - see make train, below; the stages not rendered yet listed as waiting), and marks new samples until they're heard. Logs has the tails of make train's 12 most recently updated logs (all of them with "Show older logs", only those updated in the last 3 minutes with "Active only"; "Expand" shows a log's last 500 lines, the full width). It refreshes every second, read-only, re-fetching only what changed, and reloads itself when its own code changes.

The listener panel

Neural listener models score the synthesizer the way a panel of listeners would - a repeatable check of naturalness, speaker identity, and speaking style alongside Whisper's intelligibility. They run in a Python environment of their own (PyTorch), used only by the tools - the package has no dependencies.

Listener Model Measures
Intelligibility Whisper base.en (whisper.cpp's whisper-cli - installed separately, e.g. brew install whisper-cpp; the model downloaded, pinned, into downloads/whisper/ - picovox_train/corpora.py) Word error rate against the text
Naturalness UTMOS22 strong (through torch.hub tarepan/SpeechMOS) Predicted mean opinion score, 1-5
Speaker identity ECAPA-TDNN (SpeechBrain, VoxCeleb) A 192-dimension speaker embedding

picovox_train/panel.py is the panel's scoring, shared by every evaluation (picovox_train.distill eval, make train's gate): an exported model speaks a fixed evaluation set, tools/panel-texts.json - six Harvard sentences, six narrative passages from public-domain books with dialogue (Austen, Melville, Dickens, Doyle, Potter, Grahame), two questions, and two exclamations - through tools/speak.js (every word pronounced by the G2P network, as the package pronounces), and the renderings are scored with UTMOS, Whisper's WER, and the ECAPA speaker similarity.

picovox_train/listeners.py is the scoring tool itself - WAV files (16-bit PCM) of any sample rate in, one JSON line per file out:

build/venv-train/bin/python -I train/src/picovox_train/listeners.py [--no-mos] [--no-embedding] [--device auto|cpu|mps] \
    [--files-from list.txt|-] file.wav ...

{"file": "a.wav", "seconds": 2.63, "mos": 3.33, "embedding": [31.48, ...]}

The models load once per run (4-7 s) and then score about 35 seconds of speech a second (6 sentences) on an M3 Max's GPU (MPS, the default on Apple silicon - about 3x the CPU, with the same scores). Files longer than 20 s are scored in chunks. The models download on first use - about 1.5 GB, into downloads/listeners/ (torch.hub, Hugging Face - the training speech's phoneme recognizer, picovox_train/ctc.py's, downloads there too; like the training speech, make clean keeps them - make superclean removes them) - and later runs need no network.

The environment is built with python3 (3.14 here; 3.12 also works - make build/venv-train.build PYTHON_VENV=python3.12). Its packages are pinned in train/requirements.txt: PyTorch 2.14, torchaudio 2.11 (UTMOS's), SpeechBrain 1.1, transformers 5.20 (the phoneme recognizer's wav2vec2 - picovox_train/ctc.py), and soxr for resampling audio (WAV files are read with the standard library - picovox_train/wav.py). (The emotion2vec+ emotion recognizer, FunASR's - about 40 packages more - was the style experiments'; it's in git history.)

make train

make train recreates the generated models - lib/models/g2pModel.js and lib/models/voiceModel.js - from a clean checkout, by one recipe, train/data/train.json (every stage's settings and seeds): make train-g2p and make train-voice at once. Each model's training ends in its gate, with its module in build/train/ (g2pModel.js, voiceModel.js) - lib/models/ unchanged. The G2P is deterministic, so its gate is that it reproduces the committed module (identical below the comment header, else within the recipe's tolerances); the voice's gate is the listener panel against the shipped model. The voice data is built with the committed G2P (a G2P installed that differs makes the next make train rebuild the voice).

The prerequisites: Ollama and its TTS model, the first time - make train's training speech is spoken by a local TTS model (train/data/train.json's tts.engine - phonon, pinned by digest; see the training speech), so the first run needs Ollama running at the recipe's URL (http://localhost:11434 - the app, or ollama serve); the model is pulled if it's missing, and a model of another digest fails the stage (a new model is a new corpus). Once the corpus is generated (downloads/tts/), later runs - make train-clean too - need no Ollama. The listener panel needs whisper.cpp's whisper-cli (brew install whisper-cpp); everything else is downloaded, pinned - the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary and the panel's Whisper model by their own rules (picovox_train/corpora.py), the listener models and the phoneme recognizer on first use.

The flow:

  1. make -j train - both models, then a report (picovox_train.trained report): each model's size, the voice's listener-panel scores against the shipped model's, and whether each module differs from the committed one.
  2. The training dashboard (below) - the gates and the listening samples.
  3. make train-check - the package's tests with the trained models in place of lib/models/'s (tools/trainmodels.js, a module hook), then the blind A/B of the trained voice against the shipped one, in your browser (tools/trainab.js serves the voice lab locally, with the trained voice in build/voicelab/trained/; both sides speak with the shipped G2P, so the A/B is the voice's alone): each of the package's voices speaks the one of its candidate texts (the panel's Harvard sentences and the recipe's listening samples texts) where the two voices differ most - the distance of their parameter frames (pitch, voicing, pulse shape, tilt, the noises, the lower formants and bandwidths, each scaled to its range), aligned by dynamic time warping - so an audible difference is heard if there is one. The trials are shuffled, with 3 repeats - 15 forced choices (or Same), about 4 minutes; after 6 Same answers in a row the page offers to finish early (no audible difference). The answers are saved as you go (build/train/listen/ - a rerun resumes; a rerun after a finished round starts another, and the report counts every round). At the end it prints the report (tools/trainabreport.js): the trained voice's win rate over the decided trials with its 95% interval and sign test, the repeats' agreement, each voice's counts, the panel's scores, and a recommendation - no audible difference when at most a fifth of the answers chose a voice (keep the shipped voice: installing changes nothing you can hear), keep the shipped voice when it's significantly preferred, install the trained voice when it's preferred at least as often, else too close to call. A retrain of an unchanged recipe is usually no audible difference - the A/B is its guardrail against a bad run; a recipe change is where it decides.
  4. make train-install - the trained modules that differ into lib/models/ (the G2P when it differs below its comment header; picovox_train.trained install) - or one model, make train-install-voice or make train-install-g2p; then make commit and commit them.

The train-* targets run their commands - none decides on another's results. make train-check and make train-install fail until make train has trained both models (train-install-voice and -g2p, their model). Each stage is a stamp in build/train/ (TRAIN_DIR), so make -j train runs independent stages at once; the GPU stages run one at a time (picovox_train/gpulock.py - two at once each run at about half speed) while the CPU stages run beside them:

Project Gutenberg revises its books in place and keeps no old revisions (pg2554 changed on 2026-10-16 and moved the context labels by one example), so the pins are the texts the shipped models were trained on - the G2P's labels' copies (fetched 2026-10-15) and the first full make train's distillation copies (2026-10-16, whose pg2554 is already the revision: pg2554-2026-10-16). A text is fetched once (picovox_train/books.py - its Gutenberg URL, else its archived copy: the first with the pinned hash) and verified on every use. The fetch alone, for every pinned text: python -m picovox_train.books fetch.

make -j train-g2p runs the G2P alone - its stages (with the training speech's readings, tts-corpus and tts-readings), without the voice stages or make train's ordering after the voices' data build - for G2P work. The chain's independent parts run in worker processes - the context labels' part-of-speech tags (by book) in G2P_JOBS, and the context network's trainings and cross-validate's ten folds (each one thread) in CONTEXT_JOBS - and the context network and its cross-validation run beside the G2P network; every output is the same for any count. Measured at the background QoS (the efficiency cores only, beside another job's load - October 2026): the context labels took 36 minutes (65 before: the tags are made by book in worker processes, not by spaCy's worker pool for each book), and the ten folds 54 minutes at once (one fold alone, two threads, took 134). Distilling the voice model

Each retrain of the multi-speaker acoustic model ends the same way: train a big teacher, then distill small students from it and ship the smallest one that keeps the teacher's quality. picovox_train/distill.py runs that pipeline, with its settings in train/data/train.json's distill (the teacher, the distillation set, the training settings, the student ladder, the polish, the evaluation voices, and the tolerance):

PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.distill ladder                  # everything below, in order
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.distill targets                 # 1. the teacher's targets (about 10 minutes on the GPU)
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.distill train --student s64     # 2. each student of the ladder
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.distill polish --student s64    # 3. optional: a short fine-tune on the training speech
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.distill eval teacher s96 s64 s48 s32   # 4. each model through the browser path
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.distill pick                    # 5. speed, samples, and the smallest within tolerance
  1. Targets - sequence-level distillation in parameter space. The teacher speaks (tokens, speaker) to its durations (pauses included) and its 32 synthesizer parameters every 5 ms, as inference does, with no audio rendered. The inputs are the training utterances with their own voices, and every sentence of the recipe's 22 Project Gutenberg books (distill.targets texts - pinned texts, train/data/books.json - in build/nn/books/; tokenized by picovox_train/frontend.py, split into sentences as inference splits text) spoken twice, each time by a random one of the twelve voices. The set goes to build/nn/distill/targets/ (frames as float16).
  2. Students - the same MultiModel with smaller widths and depths. They share the teacher's conditioning: its speaker and channel-equalizer tables (a row per voice) are copied and frozen, so a voice is the same voice in a student. A student runs with the teacher's durations (teacher forcing on its length regulator) and learns L1 on the normalized parameter frames plus the duration losses against the teacher's duration outputs. The training (train/data/train.json's distill.train) is 12,000 steps of Muon on the hidden matrices (AdamW for the rest) with a warmup-stable-decay schedule - constant, then the last 25% cooled down by 1 - sqrt (picovox_train/optim.py). Runs go to build/nn/runs/distill-<student>/.
  3. Polish - picovox_train/trainmulti.py's real-audio losses (the spectral loss through the differentiable synthesizer on the training speech's clips, the adversarial critic - started from the teacher's - and feature matching) for a short fine-tune.
  4. Evaluation - each model is exported (picovox_train/export.py), packaged (picovox_train/package.py, the generated module's bytes and gzip bytes), and spoken by tools/speak.js on tools/panel-texts.json in the twelve voices (distill.eval speakers); the renderings are scored with UTMOS, Whisper's WER, and the ECAPA speaker similarity to the teacher's rendering of the same voice. pick measures tools/speak.js's real-time factor, renders listening samples to build/nn/distill-samples/, and chooses the smallest student within the tolerance - mean UTMOS within 0.05 of the teacher's, WER at most 1 point worse, and speaker similarity at least 0.85 - writing build/nn/distill/summary.json. With --against baseline (make train's gate) the basis is instead the shipped model - eval baseline=lib/models/voiceModel.js speaks the packaged module, as the package loads it (tools/unpack.js) - so a new student must be no worse than what ships, and a better teacher never fails it.

Progress lines go to build/nn/distill/ (targets.log, students.log, eval.log).

Training the G2P model

PicoVox pronounces every word with one small network, lib/models/g2pModel.js (lib/g2p.js runs it; picovox_train/g2pmodel.py is its bit-exact Python port, which pronounces the voice model's training data). picovox_train/g2p.py trains it, with the recipe in train/data/train.json's g2p settings, and picovox_train.context export writes the module, with its context network (below):

PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.heteronyms --out build/nn/heteronyms --keep train/data/g2p-relabel.json  # its words (optional)
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.g2p train --out build/nn/g2p-train      # four stages, about 30 minutes on one CPU core
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.g2p evaluate                            # the book-token error of lib/models/g2pModel.js

The network is an autoregressive encoder-decoder (embedding 32, a bidirectional GRU of 96 over the letters, a GRU decoder of 192 with attention; 369,767 parameters). It trains on every word of the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary (its first pronunciation) but a 2,000-word monitoring set, sampled half uniformly and half by the square root of the word's count in 128 Project Gutenberg books, plus - 30% of the samples - 100,812 words the dictionary lacks, labeled by a large teacher ensemble or by the spelling and -s rules where they apply (train/data/g2p-words.json). A base stage (20,000 steps of 512 at learning rate 3e-3) is polished twice with more of the samples on the most frequent words (5,000 steps at 1e-3 over the top 20,000 words, then 5,000 at 5e-4 over the top 12,000). The last stage (focus, 3,000 steps) teaches the spoken pronunciations of 56 words whose dictionary-first pronunciation isn't the usual reading (lead L IY1 D, live L IH1 V, wind W IH1 N D, ... - train/data/g2p-relabel.json; a stage's relabel replaces those words' targets, the rule-labeled words derived from them follow, and focusBoost gives them 2% of the samples). The export quantizes to 6 bits per weight. Training on the CPU with one thread is deterministic: the recipe reproduces the shipped model's weights bit for bit.

picovox_train/heteronyms.py chooses the relabeled words from the training speech's measured readings (picovox_train/measure.py - below): for each word with several dictionary readings, the reading the voices used; a word is relabeled when the voices' majority isn't the first (significant - the Wilson 95% lower bound above one half, from 3 or more books, the independent unit: the voices share one front end), the difference is lexical (a stressed vowel's quality, or the primary stress moving with it - not vowel reduction or stress alone), and it isn't a function word. The committed words were chosen from the LibriTTS-R readers' usage before 2.0 (their evidence is in the file); with --keep (make train's heteronyms stage), a committed word the training speech can't judge - its readings unmeasured, or no significant majority either way - is kept, and one whose first reading the voices significantly prefer is dropped, and the stage checks the result reproduces train/data/g2p-relabel.json.

The measured readings (python -m picovox_train.measure, make train's tts-readings stage, on the GPU - in the corpus's readings/): each heteronym token in the training speech (a measured word, by its clip's alignment) is measured by the kind of difference between its readings - where they differ in a full vowel's quality or a consonant, the phoneme recognizer's vote (picovox_train/ctc.py - each reading's CTC likelihood, with the neighboring words' phones, on the word's segment; S against Z with each only its own token); where the recognizer can't tell them apart (a reduced vowel against a full one, the vowels it merges), the vowel's formants (F1 and F2, normalized per voice, nearest of the voice's own reference vowels); the noun/verb stress pairs aren't measured. Each method is gated by controls - words of one pronunciation with a phone swapped, whose votes are the method's false votes: a method's readings are used only when at most 3% of 100 or more control votes are wrong. The measured readings are the G2P's heteronym evidence - the relabeled words, the context labels (below), and the dictionary audit - and the voice's training tokens take the measured reading where the recognizer's vote differs from the front end's.

The dictionary audit

The network learns the dictionary's errors too (HOBBES HH AA1 B IY0 Z - "hobbies" - and, from it, "mobbes" M AA1 B IY2 Z), so train/data/g2p-fixes.json corrects the dictionary's labels where the evidence says they're wrong; g2p.py applies it in every stage, before the relabeled words (the rule-labeled words derived from a fixed word follow - HOBBES'S HH AA1 B Z IH0 Z). picovox_train/g2paudit.py finds them:

PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.g2paudit folds --out build/nn/g2paudit -j 5     # 10 screening models (resumable)
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.g2paudit suspects --out build/nn/g2paudit \
    --books build/nn/books --catalog ../picoshelf/data/catalog.json --heteronyms build/nn/context/labels.json
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.g2paudit audio --out build/nn/g2paudit [--speak]
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.g2paudit adjudicate --out build/nn/g2paudit

Two partitions of the dictionary into five folds (a word's possessives in its fold) train ten screening models (the recipe's network and sampling, 6,000 steps, no rule- or teacher-labeled words), so every word is held out by two of them (held-out accuracy 62% - any pronunciation, stress included; the dictionary is mostly names). A word is a suspect when both its models' phones differ from its label (not by stress alone) by a log-probability margin of 3, or both prefer another of its variants by that margin: 12,750 of 124,101 words - mostly legitimate irregulars (of, colonel) and foreign names. Where the training speech's voices spoke a suspect (with --speak, a word of too few tokens has sentences of the books with it spoken by the TTS model, in turn by the voices, and aligned), the CTC phoneme recognizer (picovox_train/ctc.py) scores the label and each alternative (the models' predictions, the dictionary's variants - those that would be a lexical correction: not stress or reduction alone, not a consonant dropped, not a merger's vowels, not a function word or a one- or two-letter word) on up to 12 of its aligned tokens with their neighbors; a token votes for a candidate 2 (log) more likely than the rest, and a confirmed error needs the votes' Wilson 95% lower bound above one half from 3 or more books. Control words (both models agree with the label, against a model's second guess) measure the method. The TTS model's pronunciation of a rare word or a name is its own G2P's guess, so a verdict is the engine and the fold models together. (The committed fixes were found before 2.0 with the LibriTTS-R readers' recordings: 140 control words, no false errors and 1.5% of the tokens' votes for the wrong candidate; the audio confirmed 13 errors and 143 labels, the rest without audio evidence.)

The names regression set (train/data/names-test.json, run by tools/heteronyms.js as kind names): surnames, first names, and place names with verified pronunciations - HOBBES first (the dictionary's own evidence: its second variant, HOBBS, and every other -bes word), the audio's confirmed errors and labels, and well-established names. Its minimum is the shipped module's score when it was added (63/73), so it can only go up.

The probes (kind probe in train/data/heteronyms-test.json): both readings of the context network's thin words - polish, contract, winds, bass, minute, and invalid - in 38 sentences written apart from the labels' seeds and collocations, so the context set's few sentences aren't the only measure of how the network generalizes (the regression set's sentences, the probes too, are never training examples). 1.6.8's module scores 26 of 38, 2.0's 34; the minimum is 0.85.

Heteronyms

Some words have readings their neighbors decide - "I read it yesterday" and "I will read it", "their lives" and "she lives", "a lead pipe", the noun/verb stress pairs. The G2P module also holds two small pieces for them (no word list or rule at runtime): the G2P takes a reading index (a learned vector added to its decoder's first state; reading 0 is the default) and has an ambiguity head (whether the word's reading depends on its context); for an ambiguous word, a context network (picovox_train/context.py: a character GRU over the three words and phrase-end marks each side, a bidirectional GRU, and a layer gated by the word itself - 47,779 parameters at 6 bits) chooses the reading.

The context network is distilled from an ensemble, for steadiness: one network's choices where its labels are thin depend on its seed (October 2026: five seeds of the same labels differed on 14% of unlabeled book windows and 9 of the regression set's cases a pair, and adding a few seeds for one word flipped others). So four teacher networks (their own seeds) are trained first, and the shipped network - the same size - is trained toward their mean probabilities on its examples and on unlabeled transfer windows (the ambiguous words' other windows in the books), with context words dropped at random (itemDropout).

The settings are train/data/train.json's context section. make train-g2p runs all of it (the voice stages use the committed lib/models/g2pModel.js) and gates its module with picovox_train.context check: identical to the committed module below the comment header (the stages are deterministic on the same inputs), else within the recipe's tolerances (the regression set, the book error, the top-10K, the monitoring WER) - then make train-install replaces the committed module only when it differs. The books are pinned (train/data/books.json - see make train), so a clean run reproduces the committed module.

The labels (picovox_train/contextlabels.py, with the curated part in train/data/context-labels.json) come from three sources: the training speech's measured readings (each candidate word's token whose reading the recognizer's vote or the formants measured - the voice's reading, heard in the audio); part-of-speech tags over the Project Gutenberg books (spaCy, training time only - train/requirements.txt), mapped to readings only for words whose mapping agrees with the measured readings on 95% of 10 or more tokens (a stress-pair or other family word of too few, by its family's mapping on 200 or more); and hand-written seed sentences and reviewed collocations for the readings the tags can't separate (lead the metal, bass, bow, row, does the deer, house the verb) and the pairs the audio doesn't validate - a collocation's token is its example, not a measured one, and the corpus's disagreements with the collocations (the TTS model's own reading errors) are reported. Two books and the regression set's sentences are held out - every source's examples of them, the book the independent unit - and the held-out accuracy gate (picovox_train.context gate, folds) is cross-validated by book.

The training speech

The voice network's training speech isn't downloaded - make train makes it: picovox_train/tts.py has a local TTS model speak sentences of the pinned Project Gutenberg books through Ollama's speech endpoint (POST /v1/audio/speech - 24 kHz WAV), one of the model's voices for each of the package's voices, then measures the clips with our own tools. Its settings are train/data/train.json's tts section: the engine (its URL, model, and pinned digest), the voices, the corpus's size, the coverage quotas, the split, and the checks.

PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.tts generate             # the selection and the clips (Ollama - resumable)
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.tts align                # the clips' CTC alignment (GPU - the changed clips only)
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.measure                  # the heteronym tokens' readings (GPU)
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.tts build --out DIR      # the voices' training data

  • The selection - book sentences through our front end, each sentence's text its normalized words and phrase marks, so the engine speaks exactly the words the voice network's tokens spell (the panel's and the listening samples' texts excluded). First the heteronym coverage - the context gate's words, the context labels' candidate words, and the other measured words, each in up to its quota of sentences (200, 24, and 12) from the context labels' books, the rarest word first - then sentences at random from the distillation's books, to 4 hours of speech a voice (about 48 hours). Each clip's voice and split (5% test, 5% validation) are by a hash of its text.
  • The alignment - a wav2vec2 phoneme recognizer (facebook/wav2vec2-lv-60-espeak-cv-ft - espeak IPA, 20 ms frames; picovox_train/ctc.py) force-aligns our phones to each clip (each phone the sum of the IPA tokens it may be heard as, a Viterbi pass over the CTC topology). Checked against CMU ARCTIC's EHMM phone labels (300 utterances of slt and bdl - the check is in git history), its phone onsets were within 20 ms 68% of the time and within 40 ms 93%, a mean absolute error of 18 ms.
  • The checks - the recognizer's greedy phones against the sentence's: a clip cut short (its last phones missing) or garbled (a phone error above 35% - a misread or hallucinated clip) is left out of the training data, as is one clipped at full scale or with a phone of no frames.
  • The readings - each heteronym token's reading, measured (picovox_train/measure.py - see Training the G2P model).

The corpus is cached in downloads/tts/<model>-<digest>/ - the selection, the clips (wav/<voice>/<id>.wav), their alignments, and the measured readings - so it is generated once: with it there, make train needs no Ollama, and the tts-corpus and tts-readings stages take a minute. A clip's id is a hash of its engine voice and text, so a bigger corpus or another selection only adds clips. make clean keeps it; make superclean removes it with the rest of downloads/ (about 10 GB in all) - and since the engine isn't deterministic, a corpus generated again is new speech, and make train no longer reproduces the committed voice model.

Another local TTS model is a change of the recipe alone: set tts.engine (the model, its digest - the first 12 hex digits of its digest in ollama list - and the URL) and tts.voices (an engine voice and its sex for each of the package's voice keys, the default first), and the next make train generates a new corpus (a new model or digest is a new directory) and retrains the voice from it; the voices' descriptions (lib/synthesis.js, test/voices.json) follow what they sound like. The engine is reached through Ollama's API alone - its model list and pull, and the speech endpoint (a model, a voice, and text in; a WAV out).

Phonon's English checkpoint and its voices are under the Apache License 2.0 (ollama.com/library/phonon, huggingface.co/gradium/phonon-voices); its training data is undocumented. The training speech is used locally, at train time - it isn't distributed; the package holds only the voice network trained on it.

make train's other downloads are pinned files, fetched by picovox_train/corpora.py - idempotently and sequentially, resuming interrupted downloads, each checked against its SHA-256 (--list shows them): the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary (downloads/cmudict/) and the listener panel's Whisper model (downloads/whisper/):

make build/venv-train.build                                         # build/venv-train (corpora.py needs only the standard library)
build/venv-train/bin/python -I train/src/picovox_train/corpora.py   # the dictionary and the Whisper model into downloads/

(The human speech corpora the voice model was trained on before 2.0 - CMU ARCTIC, LibriTTS-R with LibriTTSLabel's alignments, and CREMA-D - and the speaker pool, train/data/speakers.json, are in git history.)
