PicoVox

PicoVox - realistic speech synthesis in pure JavaScript, under 1 MB.

The paper: PicoVox: Realistic Neural Speech Synthesis in Under 1 MB of JavaScript - how PicoVox's networks are designed, trained, and evaluated.

PicoVox speaks English text with twelve natural-sounding male and female voices, entirely on your device - in the browser or in Node. There is no recorded speech, no server, no WebAssembly, and no runtime dependencies: a small grapheme-to-phoneme network pronounces every word, a small neural network (283K parameters) predicts interpretable source-filter synthesizer parameters every 5 ms, and a JavaScript synthesizer with an LF glottal source renders them. The whole package - code and both networks - is under 1 MB.

PicoShelf, every public-domain book read aloud right in your browser, is built on PicoVox.

Installation

npm install picovox

PicoVox is ESM-only. It runs in current browsers (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari 16.4 or later) and in Node 22 or later.

Quick start

In the browser - speak text aloud with Web Audio (call it from a click, so the browser lets the audio start):

<button id="speak">Speak</button>
<script type="module">
    import {speak} from './node_modules/picovox/lib/index.js';

    document.getElementById('speak').onclick = () => {
        speak('It was the best of times, it was the worst of times.', {'voice': 'female'});
    };
</script>

(With a bundler or an import map, import from 'picovox'.)

In Node - synthesize speech and write a WAV file:

import {synthesize, toWav} from 'picovox';
import {writeFileSync} from 'node:fs';

const {samples, sampleRate} = await synthesize('Call me Ishmael.', {'voice': 'male'});
writeFileSync('ishmael.wav', toWav(samples, sampleRate));

Streaming - a sentence and a fraction of a second at a time, the first speech in about 70 ms (M3 Max):

import {stream} from 'picovox';

for await (const block of stream(longText, {'voice': 'female', 'rate': 1.2})) {
    // block is a Float32Array of 24 kHz samples - play it, or post it from a Web Worker
}

API

synthesize(text, options)

Synthesize speech. Returns a Promise of {samples, sampleRate} - samples is a Float32Array of mono samples (-1 to

  1. at sampleRate Hz.

stream(text, options)

Synthesize speech as a stream - an async iterable of Float32Array blocks of samples, made as they're taken. The text is spoken a sentence at a time, each sentence a fraction of a second at a time, so the first audio comes almost at once. The blocks, concatenated, are exactly synthesize's samples. Synthesis runs on the calling thread as each block is taken - in a browser, take the blocks in a Web Worker to keep the page responsive.

speak(text, options)

Speak text aloud in a browser, with Web Audio. Returns a playback, {stop(), finished} - stop() stops it, and finished is a Promise that resolves when the playback ends or is stopped (and rejects if synthesis fails). Playback starts with the first blocks while the rest are synthesized, a few seconds ahead. Synthesis runs on the main thread, a block at a time between the page's other work; for long texts, use stream in a Web Worker and play its blocks. options.context is an AudioContext to play in (by default, a new one at the sample rate, closed when the playback ends). Play blocks in a context at their sample rate (new AudioContext({'sampleRate': 24000})): a context at another rate resamples each block apart, and the blocks' joins click. Under Node, speak throws an error - there is no Web Audio; use synthesize and toWav.

Each voice is one row of the voice model's speaker table, learned from one voice of the local TTS model that speaks make train's training speech (train/data/train.json's tts voices - see the training speech); test/voices.json is their design record. The voices are the model's speakers as it speaks them - no blends, and no pitch, tract, or rate adjustments of their own - except that every voice's source is 5 dB darker than the network predicts it (lib/synthesis.js sourceShelf): in blind A/B listening (1.6.6) the network's source sounded tinny, and darker it was less tinny and never less natural. On the listener panel's texts the voices have a Whisper word error rate of 0.9-1.2% (1.06% over the twelve) and a gap to their TTS source of 2,054 (fdL12 - the listener panel) - the same within a training seed's spread as 2.0.1's, which they replaced (0.98% and 2,068; a blind A/B heard no difference in 6 trials of 6). 2.0.1's scored a mean UTMOS of 4.42, 2.0.0's 4.31 (and 1.13%), and the voices before 2.0 4.13 and 1.36% (a blind A/B preferred 2.0's voices in all 15 trials).

styles()

The speaking styles - [{key, label, kind, description}], neutral (the default) first. Any style goes with any voice:

Key Kind
neutral (the default) emotion The voice as designed - plain narration
angry emotion A pressed, raised, louder voice
happy emotion A brighter, much higher voice
sad emotion A quieter, lower, flatter, and slower voice
fearful emotion A breathy, quieter, much higher, and quicker voice
disgusted emotion A pressed, quieter, lower, and much slower voice
whisper mode No voicing - the breath only
soft mode A quieter voice, a little slower
shouting mode A pressed, bright, raised voice, a little louder
dialogue mode Neutral dialogue - a little higher, brighter, and quicker than the narration
exclaim mode An exclamation - the dialogue style livelier: louder, wider in pitch, and quicker

Every style transforms the synthesizer's parameters (lib/transforms.js - a pressed or relaxed glottal pulse, pitch and pitch range, whisper, level, frication) and the pace; the voice model has no style input. The emotions' transforms are measured: before 2.0 the voice model had emotion embeddings learned from CREMA-D's actors, and happy, sad, fearful, and disgusted are those embeddings' effects on the parameter frames (pitch, pitch range, pulse, voicing, frication, level, and duration, over the panel texts in every voice), less the breathiness they added; angry was transforms alone already. On the listener panel's texts, in the female and male voices, every style stays intelligible (Whisper's word error rate 0.6-3.0%, whispering 2.4-3.9%); UTMOS - a judge of plain read speech - scores every voiced style within 0.05 of neutral's (4.40 and 4.26) but fearful (0.08 and 0.15 below), and can't score a whisper; and every style but whisper (6.5-9.7 dB quieter) and soft (5 dB) is within 4.1 dB of neutral's loudness - angry 3.3-3.8 dB louder, sad 3.4-3.6 quieter.

Expressive reading: style: 'auto'

Style 'auto' reads a text's dialogue as a narrator does - its delivery follows the text's quotations:

  • The dialogue beat. The quotations (between double quotation marks) are read in the dialogue style - a little higher, brighter, and quicker than the narration (pitch +7%, pace +5%) - and the narration neutral.
  • The exclamation beat. A quoted sentence ending in "!" is read in the exclaim style - livelier than the dialogue beat: 3 dB louder, its pitch range 20% wider, a little more pressed and quicker - excited, not shouted.

Style 'auto' needs no model - it reads the text's quotation marks and exclamation marks. (A small network that would read a quotation angry, shouting, or fearful was tried and dropped: even a classifier on a 4-billion-parameter embedding model couldn't choose those styles reliably enough - see the paper's Section 12.7.)

On the listener panel's texts the beats keep the voices intelligible and natural: in all twelve voices style 'auto''s UTMOS was within 0.04 of neutral's and its word error rate at most 2.4% (neutral's 0.6-1.8%) - measured with the paragraph beat it had through 2.0.1 (each paragraph read as one arc - its first sentence lifted, its pitch settling; removed in 2.0.2: blind, it was heard as no different, and in a book it sounded like the voice changing for no reason). The output is deterministic, as every style's; without 'auto' the audio is the same as before, bit for bit.

toWav(samples, sampleRate)

Encode samples (a Float32Array, or an array of numbers, -1 to 1 - values beyond are clipped) as a 16-bit PCM mono WAV file - a Uint8Array of the file's bytes.

Options

synthesize, stream, and speak take the same options:

Option Default
voice 'male' The voice's key - see voices()
style 'neutral' The speaking style's key - see styles() - or 'auto', expressive reading (below)
rate 1 The speaking speed, from 0.5 to 2 (1.2 is 20% faster)
seed 1000 The noise seed (an integer) - sentence i of the text is rendered with seed + i
sampleRate 24000 The output sample rate (Hz), from 8000 to 96000 - 24 kHz is the voices' own rate

The text is split into sentences (and paragraphs, at blank lines); each sentence starts with a short pause - 0.24 seconds, or 0.4 before a paragraph (so the text's first sentence starts with one). Numbers (four-digit numbers as years, 1972 as "nineteen seventy-two"; ordinals, 1st; decades, 1970s; percentages; money, $1.50 and $5 million; times, 10:30 a.m.), units after a number (5 km as "five kilometers", 93,000 sq mi, 90 km/h, 240,000 km2, −5 °C), common symbols (&, +, =, ×), common abbreviations, dashes, ellipses, and Roman numerals (Chapter IV, World War II) are spoken as words, and an aside's parentheses pause as a comma does. An initial (Dr. A. B. Jones) or a dotted abbreviation (the U.S., a Ph.D., a.k.a.) is its letters' names - "i.e." is "that is", "e.g." "for example" - and ends a sentence only before a word that starts one ("in the U.S. The law", not "the U.S. Army"); a word in capitals is spelled when the G2P network finds it an acronym (AI, TOC, FBI; with its -s - CEOs, IBM's) and read when it finds it a word (NOT, STOP, NASA). An invalid text or option throws a TypeError or a RangeError with a message saying what's wrong (stream and speak throw at once; synthesize rejects).

The output is bit-for-bit deterministic: the same text and options give the same samples, in the browser and in Node.

How the models are trained

Both networks are trained from scratch by make train, which rebuilds lib/models/g2pModel.js and lib/models/voiceModel.js from a clean checkout. The voice network learns from training speech that make train generates: Project Gutenberg sentences spoken by a local TTS model (Gradium's Phonon, through Ollama - one of its voices for each of the package's twelve), aligned and checked with our own phoneme recognizer, then the small model that ships trained through a differentiable copy of the synthesizer. The G2P network learns from the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary (downloaded, pinned; it is not used at runtime), its heteronyms' readings measured in the training speech. The whole run takes about 85 minutes on an M3 Max (the training speech generated once, then cached - generating and aligning it the first time adds about an hour and a half). See make train below, and the paper, PicoVox: Realistic Neural Speech Synthesis in Under 1 MB of JavaScript, for the methods and results.

Development

This repository is the package (lib/, test/) and the training pipeline that makes its models (train/, tools/, the paper in paper/). The package is developed with javascript-build, and the training's Python code with python-build:

make commit      # the package's unit tests (100% coverage), lint, and API documentation (jsdoc) - and the training's gate
make test        # the package's unit tests (TEST='name' runs one)
make -C train commit      # the training's gate alone - its unit tests, pylint, and 100% branch coverage
make -C train test TEST=tests.test_params    # one test module of the training's
make -j train             # train both models - the G2P and the voice - each gated, in build/train/, then a report (see below)
make train-check          # the package's tests with the trained models, then the blind A/B of the trained voice
make train-install        # the trained models that differ into lib/models/ (train-install-voice, -g2p: one model)
make -j train-voice       # the voice alone; make -j train-g2p - the G2P alone, on every core

The training pipeline is a Python package, picovox_train (train/src/picovox_train/ - picovox_train/<module>.py below), and Node scripts (tools/); they use the package's modules but aren't part of it. train/ is a python-build package of its own: its unit tests (train/src/tests/) run on the CPU in a few minutes, with tiny synthetic data and no network, in python-build's environment (train/build/venv/), which installs the package with the training's pinned PyTorch and numpy (train/pyproject.toml, the same versions as train/requirements.txt). make train runs the package from its source (PYTHONPATH=train/src) in its environment, build/venv-train from train/requirements.txt - the training (PyTorch, numpy, and Praat) and spaCy (picovox_train/corpora.py's downloads need only the standard library). A training module runs the same way by hand:

PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.smoke   # every training entry point, a few steps on the CPU

The two interpreters weigh a change differently, and a JIT differently again (a layer computed in blocks is faster interpreted, and slower compiled by JavaScriptCore), so a change is timed in all four. --root DIR serves another app (PicoShelf, with its ?timing&log=URL timeline posted to build/perf/devlog.jsonl).

The multi-speaker model takes a speaker (--speaker, an id of its manifest's speakers - one per voice, in voices()'s order: 0 is male, the default); the voices' source shelf and the styles are lib/synthesis.js's, not the tool's.

The training dashboard, tools/dashboard/, follows make train as it runs: serve the repository (python3 -m http.server) and open http://localhost:8000/tools/dashboard/ (with ?train=DIR for a run in another TRAIN_DIR - another checkout's or worktree's; default build/train). It has three tabs (each linkable, e.g. #listen): Train, Listen, and Logs. Train is the make train run (the default tab while it runs): the run's state, its ETA and critical path; the CPU and GPU utilization over the run with each stage as a band (the GPU's idle time shaded, the forecast faded); every stage of train/data/train-stages.json in collapsible groups - its state (waiting, queued for the GPU, running, done, failed, stopped, or not in the run's make target, from TRAIN_DIR/stages.jsonl), its start, end, and time against the expected time, and its mean CPU and GPU use; the training runs (TRAIN_DIR/runs/, their log.jsonl, and their stages' logs) with charts of all their measurements - losses, validation, pitch error, and step times; and the gates (the heteronym and G2P checks, the listener panel's scores, and the pick). Listen plays the make train run's listening samples (each training stage's final model beside the shipped model - see make train, below; the stages not rendered yet listed as waiting), and marks new samples until they're heard. Logs has the tails of make train's 12 most recently updated logs (all of them with "Show older logs", only those updated in the last 3 minutes with "Active only"; "Expand" shows a log's last 500 lines, the full width). It refreshes every second, read-only, re-fetching only what changed, and reloads itself when its own code changes.

The listener panel

Two measures judge a voice model, repeatably, the way listeners would - one of how close it sounds to its source, one of how intelligible it is. They run in a Python environment of their own (PyTorch), used only by the tools - the package has no dependencies.

Measure Model Measures
The gap to the source The pinned wav2vec2 phoneme recognizer (facebook/wav2vec2-lv-60-espeak-cv-ft at a fixed revision, its weights' SHA-256 checked - the training speech's aligner, picovox_train/ctc.py, run by picovox_train/wav2vec2.py in plain PyTorch) fdL12 - each voice's Frechet distance between its synthesized clips' frames and its source clips' in the recognizer's layer 12; lower is closer
Intelligibility Whisper base.en (whisper.cpp's whisper-cli - installed separately, e.g. brew install whisper-cpp; the model downloaded, pinned, into downloads/whisper/ - picovox_train/corpora.py) Word error rate against the text

The gap to the source (picovox_train.quality gap [--model MODULE]) speaks 40 held-out clips a voice with the package (tools/renderLines.js - the voices, gain, and limiter as the package speaks) and gives each voice's Frechet distance to its source clips - the training speech's, the same texts - in the recognizer's layer 12 (and layer 6). A second take of the engine's own clips measures about 1,500, 2.0.0's voices about 2,500, 2.0.1's about 2,050. It was chosen from twenty candidates by checks written first - and it ranks 2.0.0's voice closer than the simpler recipe's retrained voice in all twelve voices, as blind listening did (the paper's Section 13.9). It decides between recipes; the seeds' spread is about 1.3% of it.

Intelligibility. picovox_train/panel.py is the panel's scoring and make train's gate (picovox_train.panel eval and gate): an exported model speaks a fixed evaluation set, tools/panel-texts.json - six Harvard sentences, six narrative passages from public-domain books with dialogue (Austen, Melville, Dickens, Doyle, Potter, Grahame), two questions, and two exclamations - through tools/speak.js (every word pronounced by the G2P network, as the package pronounces), and Whisper transcribes the renderings. Whisper is the one judge independent of the recognizer the perceptual loss trains against. (The UTMOS naturalness predictor and the ECAPA speaker similarity, the panel's listener models through 2.0.2, are in git history: UTMOS rated voices level with their source that listening heard behind, and the similarity to the shipped voices stood in the way of a voice moving toward its source.)

make train

make train recreates the generated models - lib/models/g2pModel.js and lib/models/voiceModel.js - from a clean checkout, by one recipe, train/data/train.json (every stage's settings and seeds): make train-g2p and make train-voice at once. Each model's training ends in its gate, with its module in build/train/ (g2pModel.js, voiceModel.js) - lib/models/ unchanged. The G2P is deterministic, so its gate is that it reproduces the committed module (identical below the comment header, else within the recipe's tolerances); the voice's gate is the listener panel against the shipped model. The voice data is built with the committed G2P (a G2P installed that differs makes the next make train rebuild the voice).

The prerequisites: Ollama and its TTS model, the first time - make train's training speech is spoken by a local TTS model (train/data/train.json's tts.engine - phonon, pinned by digest; see the training speech), so the first run needs Ollama running at the recipe's URL (http://localhost:11434 - the app, or ollama serve); the model is pulled if it's missing, and a model of another digest fails the stage (a new model is a new corpus). Once the corpus is generated (downloads/tts/), later runs - make train-clean too - need no Ollama. The listener panel needs whisper.cpp's whisper-cli (brew install whisper-cpp); everything else is downloaded, pinned - the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary and the panel's Whisper model by their own rules (picovox_train/corpora.py - the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary, the wav2vec2 phoneme recognizer, and the panel's Whisper model, each at a fixed revision and checked against its SHA-256).

The flow:

  1. make -j train - both models, then a report (picovox_train.trained report): each model's size, the voice's listener-panel scores against the shipped model's, and whether each module differs from the committed one.
  2. The training dashboard (below) - the gates and the listening samples.
  3. make train-check - the package's tests with the trained models in place of lib/models/'s (tools/trainmodels.js, a module hook), then the blind A/B of the trained voice against the shipped one, in your browser (tools/trainab.js serves the voice lab locally, with the trained voice in build/voicelab/trained/; both sides speak with the shipped G2P, so the A/B is the voice's alone): each of the package's voices speaks the one of its candidate texts (the panel's Harvard sentences and the recipe's listening samples texts) where the two voices differ most - the distance of their parameter frames (pitch, voicing, pulse shape, tilt, the noises, the lower formants and bandwidths, each scaled to its range), aligned by dynamic time warping - so an audible difference is heard if there is one. The trials are shuffled, with 3 repeats - 15 forced choices (or Same), about 4 minutes; after 6 Same answers in a row the page offers to finish early (no audible difference). The answers are saved as you go (build/train/listen/ - a rerun resumes; a rerun after a finished round starts another, and the report counts every round). At the end it prints the report (tools/trainabreport.js): the trained voice's win rate over the decided trials with its 95% interval and sign test, the repeats' agreement, each voice's counts, the panel's scores, and a recommendation - no audible difference when at most a fifth of the answers chose a voice (keep the shipped voice: installing changes nothing you can hear), keep the shipped voice when it's significantly preferred, install the trained voice when it's preferred at least as often, else too close to call. A retrain of an unchanged recipe is usually no audible difference - the A/B is its guardrail against a bad run; a recipe change is where it decides.
  4. make train-install - the trained modules that differ into lib/models/ (the G2P when it differs below its comment header, the voice but its manifest's provenance; picovox_train.trained install) - or one model, make train-install-voice or make train-install-g2p; then make commit and commit them.

The train-* targets run their commands - none decides on another's results. make train-check and make train-install fail until make train has trained both models (train-install-voice and -g2p, their model). Each stage is a stamp in build/train/ (TRAIN_DIR), so make -j train runs independent stages at once; the GPU stages run one at a time (picovox_train/gpulock.py - two at once each run at about half speed) while the CPU stages run beside them:

Training the voice model

The voice network ships at the size the student ladder below chose - d64: an 80-wide encoder of 3 layers and a 64-wide decoder of 4 (282,627 parameters, 311 KB gzipped). make train trains it directly on the training speech (picovox_train/trainmulti.py, the recipe's voice - its defaults.config the size), in two stages:

  1. Stage A - the spectral loss through the differentiable synthesizer on the clips (and the duration and F0 losses), the first 15,000 steps of a 30,000-step cosine schedule (10,000 for a week of November 2026 - each seed scored within the seeds' spread, but with the distillation's removal the voices averaged 0.03 UTMOS below the shipped voice, and the blind A/B leaned to it).
  2. Stage B - from A's last checkpoint, with the adversarial critic and feature matching against the over-smoothing, 10,000 steps of a warmup-stable-decay schedule (constant, then the last 25% cooled down by 1 - sqrt - picovox_train/optim.py); its last checkpoint is the voice (the validation loss doesn't pick the better voice - the paper's Sections 7.3 and 11.4 - so no best checkpoint is kept).

Both stages add the perceptual loss (picovox_train/perceptual.py, weight 5): each step's first 4 rendered crops against their recordings in the pinned wav2vec2 phoneme recognizer's layers 3 and 6 (frozen), the mean cosine distance of their frames. Chosen by the gap measure (picovox_train.quality gap) and checked by listener models it never trained against, it brought the voices a fifth closer to their source (fd L12 2,051, against 2.0.0's distilled voice's 2,522 and 2,700-2,730 without it), as close in the speaker embedding (0.278 against 0.277), and the panel's UTMOS from 4.31 to 4.42 - two seeds alike (paper Section 13.10). It makes a step about 1.6 times as long; in blind A/B listening against 2.0.0's voice, no audible difference (6 of 6 trials Same).

The recipe's other parts were put to the same test in November 2026 (the paper's Section 13.11), each retrained against seed controls and judged by the gap: the critic earns its time (stage B without it measured 2,104 and 2,135 against the controls' 2,067 and 2,093, for a step 31% faster); the perceptual loss's recognizer in bfloat16 gained nothing and ran slower; and the formant guide - log F1-F3 against Praat's, faded over stage A - made no difference (both stages without it 2,046 and 2,082 against 2,067 and 2,068), so it is gone, and with it Praat's formant analysis of the training speech (the data's tracks are F0 and voicing).

The gate (picovox_train/panel.py): the shipped model (panel eval baseline=lib/models/voiceModel.js - the packaged module spoken as the package loads it, tools/unpack.js; evaluated once per shipped model, its result cached with the training speech) and the voice (panel eval voice=CHECKPOINT - exported, packaged, and spoken by tools/speak.js on tools/panel-texts.json in the twelve voices, and by the package on 40 held-out clips a voice for the gap) through the listener panel, then panel gate voice --against baseline: the voice's WER at most 1 point worse than the shipped model's, and its mean gap to the source (fdL12) at most 2% above the shipped model's - about one and a half times the seeds' spread (the recipe's panel tolerance) - build/train/eval/summary.json, the exit status 1 if not.

Training the G2P model

PicoVox pronounces every word with one small network, lib/models/g2pModel.js (lib/g2p.js runs it; picovox_train/g2pmodel.py is its bit-exact Python port, which pronounces the voice model's training data). picovox_train/g2p.py trains it, with the recipe in train/data/train.json's g2p settings, and picovox_train.context export writes the module, with its context network (below):

PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.heteronyms --out build/nn/heteronyms --keep train/data/g2p-relabel.json  # its words (optional)
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.g2p train --out build/nn/g2p-train      # four stages, about 50 minutes on one CPU core
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.g2p evaluate                            # the book-token error of lib/models/g2pModel.js

The network is an autoregressive encoder-decoder (embedding 32, a bidirectional GRU of 128 over the letters, a GRU decoder of 256 with attention; 631,207 parameters). It trains on every word of the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary (its first pronunciation) but a 2,000-word monitoring set, sampled half uniformly and half by the square root of the word's count in the texts listeners hear - 128 Project Gutenberg books, the listeners' books, and 784 Wikipedia articles (train/data/g2p-books.json: a book's counts join the weights, times its weight - Wikipedia's 2.7, so its 5.9 million tokens weigh as the books' 16 million do; python -m picovox_train.g2p book counts a text) - plus - 30% of the samples - 100,812 words the dictionary lacks, labeled by the spelling and -s rules where they apply, else by Wiktionary where it has the word (9,332), else by a large teacher ensemble (train/data/g2p-words.json); Wiktionary also labels 7,499 of the articles' words outside the dictionary, and 911 of the most frequent of the rest are labeled by hand - names and terms (Quran, Mughal, eukaryotes) (train/data/g2p-books.json's labels - dictionary words in training). A base stage (20,000 steps of 512 at learning rate 3e-3) is polished twice with more of the samples on the most frequent words (5,000 steps at 1e-3 over the top 20,000 words, then 5,000 at 5e-4 over the top 12,000). The last stage (focus, 13,000 steps at 5e-4, sampled as the base stage is - at that length a share on the most frequent words only costs the rest) also teaches the spoken pronunciations of 56 words whose dictionary-first pronunciation isn't the usual reading (lead L IY1 D, live L IH1 V, wind W IH1 N D, ... - train/data/g2p-relabel.json; a stage's relabel replaces those words' targets, the rule-labeled words derived from them follow, and focusBoost gives them 2% of the samples). The export quantizes to 7 bits per weight, rounding each matrix by GPTQ - error-aware: a row is rounded a weight at a time, each weight's rounding error compensated in the row's later weights through the layer's input covariance (8,192 training words, a fixed draw) - so the layers' outputs, not the weights, stay close: on four seeds the export reads 23 more of 2.4 million book words wrong than the float network, and the seeds bracket zero. Training on the CPU with one thread is deterministic: the recipe reproduces the shipped model's weights bit for bit.

The measures (python -m picovox_train.g2p evaluate, and make train's gate): the book-token error - the 22 evaluation books' dictionary-word tokens pronounced differently from the label - the top 10,000 book words wrong, the monitoring set's word error, the held-out words' error (train/data/g2p-books.json's heldOut - 507 of Wikipedia's words that never train, with Wiktionary's pronunciations; the two dictionaries' conventions aside - not gated), and each evaluation text's token error (the file's eval - 215 Wikipedia articles held out of the weights; g2p book --eval adds one). The network of 128 units with Wikipedia in its weights and Wiktionary's and the hand labels (2026-11-11) against 2.0.3's of 96 with the books alone: the books 0.008% (0.049%), top-10K wrong 0 (3), held-out WER 55.4% (61.5%), monitoring WER 42.1% (41.1% - a seed's draw: four seeds of the recipe ended at 41.9% to 43.0%, so the gate's tolerance is 1.5 points), and Wikipedia 0.090% (0.286% before the hand labels, whose words the measure counts; before them 0.078%, against 2.0.3's 1.87%) - at 596 KB gzipped (360 KB), and 1.7 ms for a word it hasn't yet pronounced (1.6 ms; in Node without a JIT, a 23-word sentence's words 0.70 s, 0.74 s - once a word; as 2.0.4 shipped, before its kernels read their arrays less, 2.9 ms and 1.29 s).

picovox_train/heteronyms.py chooses the relabeled words from the training speech's measured readings (picovox_train/measure.py - below): for each word with several dictionary readings, the reading the voices used; a word is relabeled when the voices' majority isn't the first (significant - the Wilson 95% lower bound above one half, from 3 or more books, the independent unit: the voices share one front end), the difference is lexical (a stressed vowel's quality, or the primary stress moving with it - not vowel reduction or stress alone), and it isn't a function word. The committed words were chosen from the LibriTTS-R readers' usage before 2.0 (their evidence is in the file); with --keep (make train's heteronyms stage), a committed word the training speech can't judge - its readings unmeasured, or no significant majority either way - is kept, and one whose first reading the voices significantly prefer is dropped, and the stage checks the result reproduces train/data/g2p-relabel.json.

The measured readings (python -m picovox_train.measure, make train's tts-readings stage, on the GPU - in the corpus's readings/): each heteronym token in the training speech (a measured word, by its clip's alignment) is measured by the kind of difference between its readings - where they differ in a full vowel's quality or a consonant, the phoneme recognizer's vote (picovox_train/ctc.py - each reading's CTC likelihood, with the neighboring words' phones, on the word's segment; S against Z with each only its own token); where the recognizer can't tell them apart (a reduced vowel against a full one, the vowels it merges), the vowel's formants (F1 and F2, normalized per voice, nearest of the voice's own reference vowels); the noun/verb stress pairs aren't measured. Each method is gated by controls - words of one pronunciation with a phone swapped, whose votes are the method's false votes: a method's readings are used only when at most 3% of 100 or more control votes are wrong. The measured readings are the G2P's heteronym evidence - the relabeled words and the context labels (below) - and the voice's training tokens take the measured reading where the recognizer's vote differs from the front end's.

The dictionary audit

The network learns the dictionary's errors too (HOBBES HH AA1 B IY0 Z - "hobbies" - and, from it, "mobbes" M AA1 B IY2 Z), so train/data/g2p-fixes.json corrects the dictionary's labels where the evidence says they're wrong; g2p.py applies it in every stage, before the relabeled words (the rule-labeled words derived from a fixed word follow - HOBBES'S HH AA1 B Z IH0 Z). The fixes are committed data, from a one-off audit before 2.0 (its tool, picovox_train/g2paudit.py, is in git history - commits 1e86148 and 6cec8e3).

Two partitions of the dictionary into five folds (a word's possessives in its fold) trained ten screening models (the recipe's network and sampling, 6,000 steps, no rule- or teacher-labeled words), so every word was held out by two of them (held-out accuracy 62% - any pronunciation, stress included; the dictionary is mostly names). A word was a suspect when both its models' phones differed from its label (not by stress alone) by a log-probability margin of 3, or both preferred another of its variants by that margin: 12,750 of 124,101 words - mostly legitimate irregulars (of, colonel) and foreign names. Where the LibriTTS-R readers spoke a suspect, the CTC phoneme recognizer (picovox_train/ctc.py) scored the label and each alternative (the models' predictions, the dictionary's variants - those that would be a lexical correction: not stress or reduction alone, not a consonant dropped, not a merger's vowels, not a function word or a one- or two-letter word) on up to 12 of its aligned tokens with their neighbors; a token voted for a candidate 2 (log) more likely than the rest, and a confirmed error needed the votes' Wilson 95% lower bound above one half from 3 or more speakers. Control words (both models agree with the label, against a model's second guess) measured the method: 140 control words, no false errors and 1.5% of the tokens' votes for the wrong candidate. The audio confirmed 13 errors and 143 labels, the rest without audio evidence.

The names regression set (train/data/names-test.json, run by tools/heteronyms.js as kind names): surnames, first names, and place names with verified pronunciations - HOBBES first (the dictionary's own evidence: its second variant, HOBBS, and every other -bes word), the audio's confirmed errors and labels, and well-established names. Heteronyms

Some words have readings their neighbors decide - "I read it yesterday" and "I will read it", "their lives" and "she lives", "a lead pipe", the noun/verb stress pairs. The G2P module also holds two small pieces for them (no word list or rule at runtime): the G2P takes a reading index (a learned vector added to its decoder's first state; reading 0 is the default) and has an ambiguity head (whether the word's reading depends on its context); for an ambiguous word, a context network (picovox_train/context.py: a character GRU over the three words and phrase-end marks each side, a bidirectional GRU, and a layer gated by the word itself - 47,779 parameters at 7 bits) chooses the reading. The G2P learns the reading input and the ambiguity head in a last stage (picovox_train.context train-g2p, from the trained G2P).

The context network is distilled from an ensemble, for steadiness: one network's choices where its labels are thin depend on its seed (October 2026: five seeds of the same labels differed on 14% of unlabeled book windows and 9 of the regression set's cases a pair, and adding a few seeds for one word flipped others). So four teacher networks (their own seeds) are trained first, and the shipped network - the same size - is trained toward their mean probabilities on its examples and on unlabeled transfer windows (the ambiguous words' other windows in the books), with context words dropped at random (itemDropout).

PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.contextlabels books                                 # the recipe's books (pinned texts)
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.contextlabels aligned --out build/train/context    # the training speech's measured readings
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.contextlabels tag --out build/train/context        # part-of-speech tags (spaCy)
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.contextlabels labels --out build/train/context     # the ambiguous words and examples

(tag and train-context take -j, their worker processes - the results are the same for any count. train-context trains every fold's teachers at once, then every fold's network - fold 0, the labels' own held-out books, is the context network itself - and keeps a fold's results only when they are from the same labels and settings; folds is the cross-validated gate over them.)

The settings are train/data/train.json's context section. make train-g2p runs all of it (the voice stages use the committed lib/models/g2pModel.js) and gates its module with picovox_train.context check: identical to the committed module below the comment header (the stages are deterministic on the same inputs), else within the recipe's tolerances (the regression set, the book error, the top-10K, the monitoring WER) - then make train-install replaces the committed module only when it differs. The books are pinned (train/data/books.json - see make train), so a clean run reproduces the committed module.

The labels (picovox_train/contextlabels.py, with the curated part in train/data/context-labels.json) come from three sources: the training speech's measured readings (each candidate word's token whose reading the recognizer's CTC or voicing vote measured - the voice's reading, heard in the audio); part-of-speech tags over the Project Gutenberg books (spaCy, training time only - train/requirements.txt), mapped to readings only for words whose mapping agrees with the measured readings on 95% of 10 or more tokens (a stress-pair or other family word of too few, by its family's mapping on 200 or more); and hand-written seed sentences and reviewed collocations for the readings the tags can't separate (lead the metal, bass, bow, row, does the deer, house the verb) and the pairs the audio doesn't validate - a collocation's token is its example, not a measured one, and the corpus's disagreements with the collocations (the TTS model's own reading errors) are reported. Two books and the regression set's sentences are held out - every source's examples of them, the book the independent unit - and the held-out accuracy gate (picovox_train.context folds) is cross-validated by book.

Words in capitals

A word in capitals is an acronym, spelled - "AI", "TOC", "FBI" - or a word, read - "I did NOT", "THE END", "NASA" - and which it is depends on whether its letters are a word: what the G2P network knows, and no rule does. So the front end keeps a word's capitals (lib/lexicon.js - two letters or more, all capitals) and asks the G2P's spelled head: a 96-unit layer and two logits on the encoder's final states (lib/g2p.js pronounce.spelled - 24,866 parameters). The two logits are a word's two places: alone among lowercase words ("the US government", "500 AD" - spelled), and beside words in capitals that are read ("LEAVE US ALONE", a heading - read: its neighbors in its phrase, both if it has two - not "the US NATO allies"). A spelled word is its letters' names, each pronounced by the network ("a" as "ay"); a lowercase or capitalized word is never asked, and a word in capitals with an -s ("CEOs", "IBM's") is asked of its capitals - spelled, its last letter takes the -s (the network's "o's", "m's"). An initial - a capital letter with its period ("Dr. A. B. Jones") - is its letter's name too, as a dotted abbreviation's letters are ("the U.S.", "a Ph.D."), and their periods don't end a phrase; the sentence splitter (lib/sentences.js) ends a sentence at one only before a word that starts a sentence, or after the pronoun ("said I.").

The head's labels (picovox_train/spelled.py, the recipe's context.g2p.spelled) are of letter strings, from the G2P's own data: every string of two or three letters (18,252) - read when it is a common word (in the books 20 times for two letters, once for three), unless the dictionary's first pronunciation spells it (TV), and otherwise spelled; the longer known words (208,562) - spelled when the dictionary's first pronunciation is the letters' names (HTML), or another of its pronunciations is and the word isn't in the books (NOAA), and otherwise read; and 6,000 synthetic strings of four or five consonants, spelled (the dictionary has only 57 longer spelled words). Those are the labels beside a word in capitals; alone, the same - but four common words that, alone in capitals, are acronyms (US, AD, UN, CO: train/data/g2p-capitals.json - in capitals with no neighbor in capitals in a twentieth of 999 Wikipedia articles or more, at 20 times their rate in the books) are spelled. The file's other evidence is Wiktionary's: its 96 words in capitals pronounced as their letters (UCLA) are spelled, but a common word of two or three letters (PIE). The labels' word counts are the 128 books' alone: Wikipedia writes its acronyms in capitals, and by its lowercased counts "AI" and "GPS" are common words. The head trains in the G2P's reading stage on its own batches, and - the encoder frozen there, its gradient clipped alone - changes nothing else: a module trained with it is the module trained without it, weight for weight, plus the head.

The trained head (2026-11-11) finds none of the 23,202 spelled strings read and 182 of the 210,037 words spelled (0.1% - "clio", "aeon", "angst": rare four- and five-letter words), and 6 of 1,461 monitoring-set words it never saw (0.4%). On the capitals regression set it spells all 42 acronyms (the module without a head 27 - those the G2P spells in any case) and reads all 42 shouted words. What it can't know: an acronym that looks like a word and is in no dictionary or list, and a common word that is also an acronym beyond its four - "WHO" and "IT" are read.

The training speech

The voice network's training speech isn't downloaded - make train makes it: picovox_train/tts.py has a local TTS model speak sentences of the pinned Project Gutenberg books through Ollama's speech endpoint (POST /v1/audio/speech - 24 kHz WAV), one of the model's voices for each of the package's voices, then measures the clips with our own tools. Its settings are train/data/train.json's tts section: the engine (its URL, model, and pinned digest), the voices, the corpus's size, the coverage quotas, the split, and the checks.

PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.tts generate             # the selection and the clips (Ollama - resumable)
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.tts align                # the clips' CTC alignment (GPU - the changed clips only)
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.measure                  # the heteronym tokens' readings (GPU)
PYTHONPATH=train/src build/venv-train/bin/python -B -m picovox_train.tts build --out DIR      # the voices' training data

The engine is Gradium's Phonon (phonon, digest 58f58601e4e8), and the package's voices are its Trevor (male), Harper (female), Juniper (sarah), Delia (helen), Zoey (amy), Hadley (kate), Everly (grace), Miles (james), Preston (robert), Micah (michael), Easton (david), and Russell (thomas).

  • The selection - book sentences through our front end, each sentence's text its normalized words and phrase marks, so the engine speaks exactly the words the voice network's tokens spell (the panel's and the listening samples' texts excluded). First the heteronym coverage - the context gate's words, the context labels' candidate words, and the other measured words, each in up to its quota of sentences (200, 24, and 12) from the context labels' books, the rarest word first - then sentences at random from the fill's books, to 4 hours of speech a voice (about 48 hours). Each clip's voice and split (5% test, 5% validation) are by a hash of its text.
  • The alignment - a wav2vec2 phoneme recognizer (facebook/wav2vec2-lv-60-espeak-cv-ft - espeak IPA, 20 ms frames; picovox_train/ctc.py) force-aligns our phones to each clip (each phone the sum of the IPA tokens it may be heard as, a Viterbi pass over the CTC topology). Checked against CMU ARCTIC's EHMM phone labels (300 utterances of slt and bdl - the check is in git history), its phone onsets were within 20 ms 68% of the time and within 40 ms 93%, a mean absolute error of 18 ms.
  • The checks - the recognizer's greedy phones against the sentence's: a clip cut short (its last phones missing) or garbled (a phone error above 35% - a misread or hallucinated clip) is left out of the training data, as is one clipped at full scale or with a phone of no frames.
  • The readings - each heteronym token's reading, measured (picovox_train/measure.py - see Training the G2P model).

The corpus is cached in downloads/tts/<model>-<digest>/ - the selection, the clips (wav/<voice>/<id>.wav), their alignments, and the measured readings - so it is generated once: with it there, make train needs no Ollama, and the tts-corpus and tts-readings stages take a minute. A clip's id is a hash of its engine voice and text, so a bigger corpus or another selection only adds clips. make clean keeps it; make superclean removes it with the rest of downloads/ (about 10 GB in all) - and since the engine isn't deterministic, a corpus generated again is new speech, and make train no longer reproduces the committed voice model.

Another local TTS model is a change of the recipe alone: set tts.engine (the model, its digest - the first 12 hex digits of its digest in ollama list - and the URL) and tts.voices (an engine voice and its sex for each of the package's voice keys, the default first), and the next make train generates a new corpus (a new model or digest is a new directory) and retrains the voice from it; the voices' descriptions (lib/synthesis.js, test/voices.json) follow what they sound like. The engine is reached through Ollama's API alone - its model list and pull, and the speech endpoint (a model, a voice, and text in; a WAV out).

Phonon's English checkpoint and its voices are under the Apache License 2.0 (ollama.com/library/phonon, huggingface.co/gradium/phonon-voices); its training data is undocumented. The training speech is used locally, at train time - it isn't distributed; the package holds only the voice network trained on it.

make train's other downloads are pinned files, fetched by picovox_train/corpora.py - idempotently and sequentially, each checked against its SHA-256: the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary (downloads/cmudict/) and the listener panel's Whisper model (downloads/whisper/):

make build/venv-train.build                                         # build/venv-train (corpora.py needs only the standard library)
build/venv-train/bin/python -I train/src/picovox_train/corpora.py   # the dictionary and the Whisper model into downloads/

(The human speech corpora the voice model was trained on before 2.0 - CMU ARCTIC, LibriTTS-R with LibriTTSLabel's alignments, and CREMA-D - and the speaker pool, train/data/speakers.json, are in git history.)
